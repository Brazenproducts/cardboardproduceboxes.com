Produce Box Spec Guide: Gauge, Wall Thickness, and Load Ratings Explained

Cardboardproduceboxes Editorial • September 2026

Why Specs Matter More Than Price in Produce Packaging

When procurement teams evaluate produce boxes, the line item on the quote sheet rarely tells the full story. Two boxes at the same price point can have completely different load ratings, moisture resistance, and service lives depending on their construction specs. Understanding how to read and compare those specs protects your operation from premature box failure, product damage claims, and the hidden cost of early replacement cycles.

This guide covers the core specifications for PP corrugated produce boxes: gauge, wall thickness, flute geometry, and load ratings — and what those numbers mean for real-world field and cold-chain performance.

Gauge and Sheet Thickness

In PP corrugated, gauge refers to the nominal thickness of the finished twin-wall sheet. Common gauges for produce applications range from 4mm to 10mm. Thinner sheets (4–5mm) suit lighter-duty applications like retail trays and display packs. Mid-range gauges (6–8mm) cover the majority of produce box applications — field harvest bins, retail-ready crates, and cold-chain shipping containers. Heavy-gauge sheets (9–10mm) are used in high-stack bulk applications where box-on-box compression loads are significant.

  • 4–5mm: Retail trays, display inserts, light-duty protective liners
  • 6mm: Standard produce crates, single-wall replacement for cardboard
  • 8mm: Heavy produce bins, palletized multi-stack applications
  • 10mm: Bulk containers, heavy field harvest, extended trip cycles

Gauge alone does not determine performance. The resin density and flute geometry are equally important, which is why you should always ask for both gauge and gram-per-square-meter (GSM) weight when comparing quotes.

Flute Profile and Structural Performance

PP corrugated sheets are manufactured with an internal flute structure — the parallel ribs running between the two face sheets. Flute pitch (spacing between ribs) and flute height (rib depth) determine compression strength and flex resistance. Narrow-pitch, tall-flute constructions resist vertical compression loads better. Wide-pitch, shallow-flute constructions are lighter and more flexible, suitable for applications where the box is not heavily stacked.

For produce boxes used in palletized cold-chain shipping, request flute pitch of 4–6mm and a compression strength rating of at least 800N per linear meter. This ensures the box maintains integrity under 5–7 pallet high stacking at full load.

Load Ratings: Static vs Dynamic

Produce box load ratings are typically expressed in two ways: static load (the weight the box can support sitting still) and dynamic load (performance under vibration and movement during transport). Static ratings matter for warehouse storage. Dynamic ratings matter for truck and container transit.

A box rated for 60kg static load may only reliably handle 35–40kg under vibration on a refrigerated truck. Always ask your supplier for both figures and verify them against your actual pallet build weight before committing to a spec.

Food-Contact Resin Requirements

Produce boxes in direct or incidental food contact must use FDA-listed PP resin. This applies to berry crates, leafy green bins, and any box used with a product that may contact the box wall through packaging film or mesh bags. Request the resin compliance certificate — not just a verbal assurance — for any produce application.

Quality PP corrugated produce boxes from established manufacturers include FDA resin documentation as standard. If a supplier cannot provide this, that is a disqualifying factor for food applications regardless of price.

Custom Sizing and Interior Dimensions

Standard produce box sizes (600x400, 400x300, and similar Euro footprints) work well for many applications. But growers and distributors handling non-standard produce formats — oversized squash, bundled asparagus, specialty citrus — often need custom interior dimensions to minimize headspace and protect product geometry.

The agriculture packaging from Elipacko supports custom size orders with standard lead times of 45–60 days from approved artwork. Minimum order quantities vary by gauge and configuration but are generally accessible for mid-size operations running 10,000+ units per season.

Building a Spec Sheet for Supplier Comparison

When issuing RFQs for produce boxes, include these spec parameters to ensure you are comparing equivalent products:

  • Nominal gauge (mm) and GSM weight
  • Flute pitch and height
  • Static and dynamic load ratings
  • Interior and exterior dimensions (specify which)
  • Color and UV stabilization requirement
  • FDA resin certification required: yes/no
  • Quantity per pallet for freight cost estimation

Suppliers who cannot answer all of these points in writing are likely resellers rather than manufacturers, which adds lead time risk and limits your ability to adjust specs on reorders.
