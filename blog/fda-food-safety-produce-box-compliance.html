FDA Food Safety Compliance for Produce Boxes: What Buyers Need to Know

Cardboardproduceboxes Editorial • September 2026

Why Food Safety Compliance Starts With the Box

For procurement managers sourcing packaging for fresh produce, regulatory compliance is no longer a back-office concern — it's a procurement decision. The FDA's Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA), along with USDA agricultural transport guidelines, places direct responsibility on handlers and buyers to ensure that every container in the supply chain meets hygiene and material safety standards.

Choosing the right produce box isn't just about load capacity or cost. It's about protecting your brand, your supply chain partners, and — most importantly — the end consumer.

FSMA and the Produce Safety Rule

The FDA's Produce Safety Rule under FSMA requires that packaging and containers used in the handling and transport of raw agricultural commodities must not become a source of contamination. This means packaging materials must:

  • Be made from food-safe materials that do not leach harmful substances
  • Be cleanable and sanitizable, or intended for single-use and properly disposed of
  • Resist moisture, mold, and microbial growth to prevent cross-contamination
  • Be free from cracks, crevices, and porous surfaces where pathogens can harbor

Cardboard and wax-coated boxes, while widely used, present challenges in all four areas. When wet, they absorb moisture, harbor bacteria, and cannot be effectively sanitized for reuse.

PP Corrugated Boxes and FDA-Compliant Resin Options

Polypropylene (PP) corrugated produce boxes offer a compelling compliance profile. PP is classified as FDA-compliant for direct food contact when manufactured using approved resin grades — meaning the material itself does not off-gas, leach plasticizers, or absorb food residue.

Unlike cardboard, PP corrugated surfaces are non-porous and can be cleaned with standard food-grade sanitizers between trips. For facilities operating under HACCP plans or third-party food safety audits (SQF, BRC, GlobalG.A.P.), reusable PP containers are increasingly the auditor-preferred choice because they can be documented, cleaned, and traced through each trip cycle.

Buyers sourcing PP corrugated produce boxes should request documentation confirming FDA-compliant resin grades. Reputable manufacturers supplying the US market from Thailand and Vietnam will provide material safety data sheets and resin certifications upon request.

USDA Requirements for Agricultural Transport Packaging

The USDA's agricultural marketing standards also touch packaging for graded produce. For certain commodity categories — apples, potatoes, citrus — packaging must meet minimum strength and labeling requirements under Agricultural Marketing Act programs. While these standards don't mandate a specific material, they do set expectations around structural integrity under stacking loads, an area where PP corrugated consistently outperforms wet cardboard.

For export shipments, particularly to markets with strict phytosanitary requirements such as the EU, Japan, and Korea, PP corrugated eliminates the wood packaging ISPM-15 fumigation concerns entirely, simplifying customs documentation and reducing delays.

Choosing a Compliant Supplier

When evaluating suppliers of produce packaging, procurement teams should ask:

  1. What resin grade is used? Confirm FDA-compliant PP resin for food contact applications.
  2. Are material certifications available? Request MSDS documentation and resin supplier certification letters.
  3. What are the cleaning protocols? Understand wash temperature ranges and approved sanitizer compatibility.
  4. Can the supplier provide custom sizes? Compliance sometimes requires specific box dimensions to fit cold-room racking or transport configurations.
  5. What is the import duty status? Sourcing from countries with 0% anti-dumping duties on PP packaging can significantly affect landed cost.

Building a Compliance-Ready Produce Packaging Program

For operations managers building a produce packaging specification, best practice includes establishing a written packaging specification that references FDA resin requirements and FSMA Produce Safety Rule compliance, documenting the supplier's resin certifications and keeping them on file for audits, implementing a documented wash-and-inspect cycle for reusable PP containers, and setting a clear retirement criteria — for example, retiring boxes after 50 trips or upon observation of structural damage.

Compliance is most sustainable when it's built into purchasing standards rather than added after an audit finding. Starting with the right material — food-safe, cleanable, and structurally consistent — makes the entire program more defensible and easier to document for regulators, retail customers, and food safety auditors alike.
