Cost-Per-Trip Analysis: Cardboard vs PP Corrugated Produce Boxes

Cardboardproduceboxes Editorial • September 2026

Why Cost-Per-Trip Is the Right Metric

Procurement teams evaluating produce packaging often anchor on unit price. That works fine for single-use purchases, but it obscures the actual economics when reusable options are in play. Cost-per-trip — total spend divided by the number of trips a container completes before retirement — gives you an apples-to-apples comparison between cardboard and polypropylene corrugated boxes.

This guide walks through a structured cost-per-trip model so you can build an honest business case, whether you are running a closed-loop distribution system or evaluating a partial conversion.

Cardboard Produce Box Economics

Standard wax-coated cardboard produce boxes range from $1.80 to $3.50 per unit depending on size, supplier, and order volume. They are single-use by design. In wet environments — field harvest, ice pack, retail coolers — the box integrity degrades quickly, often within a single trip. Recycling recovery rates are inconsistent, and wax-coated board is frequently rejected by recyclers entirely.

  • Unit cost: $1.80–$3.50
  • Trips per box: 1 (single use)
  • Disposal cost: $0.10–$0.30 per unit (landfill or recycling logistics)
  • Effective cost per trip: $1.90–$3.80

For operations running 500,000 units per season, that is $950,000 to $1.9 million in packaging cost alone, with no residual asset value.

PP Corrugated Produce Box Economics

Reusable PP corrugated produce boxes carry a higher upfront cost — typically $8 to $18 per unit depending on wall thickness, size, and print requirements. However, their service life changes the math entirely. Well-maintained PP corrugated boxes in produce applications typically complete 50 to 150 trips before showing structural fatigue, assuming proper cleaning and inspection protocols.

  • Unit cost: $8–$18
  • Trips per box: 50–150
  • Annual cleaning cost: $0.05–$0.15 per trip
  • Effective cost per trip: $0.11–$0.51 (at 50–150 trips)

At even the conservative end — $18 box, 50 trips, $0.15 cleaning — you land at $0.51 per trip versus a minimum of $1.90 for cardboard. That is a 73% reduction in cost per trip.

Hidden Costs That Tilt the Comparison Further

Direct unit cost and trip count are only part of the picture. Several secondary factors consistently favor PP corrugated in produce operations:

  • Labor for box setup: Cardboard boxes require assembly at line speed. Pre-formed PP boxes eliminate that step.
  • Product damage rate: Cardboard loses rigidity when wet. PP maintains wall integrity, reducing crush damage and product loss claims.
  • Storage footprint: Nestable PP boxes reduce storage cube by 60–70% compared to flat-pack cardboard, lowering warehouse space costs.
  • Regulatory pressure: Several USDA and retail customer food safety standards now require documentation of packaging material origin and food-contact compliance, favoring PP resin certifications over wax-board.

Sourcing PP Produce Boxes That Hold Up

The cost-per-trip model only works if the boxes actually complete the expected trip count. That means sourcing from manufacturers who specify resin grade, wall construction, and load ratings — not just print a nominal trip count on a spec sheet.

Building Your Own Model

To run this analysis for your operation, you need four numbers: current cardboard unit cost, annual volume, expected PP trip count from your supplier, and PP unit cost. Plug them into the cost-per-trip formula and add a cleaning cost estimate of $0.08 to $0.15 per trip for wash-line operations.

Most operations with volumes above 100,000 units per season reach payback within 1.5 to 2 seasons. Above 500,000 units, payback often lands inside a single season, making PP corrugated one of the highest-ROI packaging transitions available to produce procurement teams today.
